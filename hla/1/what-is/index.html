HLA 1 · Understand

What is a culture of high expectations?

Introduction

An often neglected aspect of school improvement is culture. When we think about improving schools, it is natural to focus on tangible things; better textbooks, brighter buildings, smaller class sizes, and better teacher training programs, to name a few. But in fact, none of those wonderful things will lead to sustained school improvement unless they are part of the right school culture. School culture is, however, rarely discussed because it is difficult to describe and quantify.

So what is culture? Put simply, culture is the set of shared beliefs, assumptions, and ways of thinking that drives a group’s behavior. Culture affects everything that happens in a school—from what people wear, to how they behave in meetings, to how they treat colleagues and students, to how they respond to change. When school leaders privately lament to their friends and family that they don’t know what’s wrong with their school, it’s often the culture that they are talking about.

Culture affects how school leaders, teachers, and students feel about school and their role in it. Culture dictates whether teachers see themselves as in competition or in collaboration. Culture explains whether students feel safe or scared. Culture influences how likely teachers are to ask for help. And culture tells students whether they should keep going, or give up.

The good news is that school culture can be shaped by the school leader into a culture that allows all students and adults to learn and grow together. While every school’s culture will be uniquely shaped by its students, staff, history and other factors, there are two things that must show in the culture of every good school. First, it should be supportive. This means that students and teachers have a sense of belonging and trust in the school, and share the belief that everyone - students and adults alike - deserves support to learn and grow. It also means that everyone feels safe, secure, and respected at school. It also means that classrooms feel safe and supportive, which requires teachers to manage behavior proactively and sensitively. Second, there should be high expectations for students. This means that everyone in the school community - students, families, teachers, and leaders are committed to the highest standards of teaching and learning. In particular, teachers have high expectations for their students and communicate their high expectations often. In summary, good schools develop a culture of high expectations balanced with a culture of support.

School Leader Mindsets

  1. School leaders are the guardians of school culture and must have a clear vision for it.
  2. All students and teachers can meet high expectations when given appropriate support.
  3. Supportive school cultures emerge when levels of trust are high, when relationships are strong, and when all community members have a voice.

School Leader Practices and Teacher Practices

LevelSchool Leader PracticesTeacher Practices
Emerging

Builds relationships with students, staff, and families by actively engaging in daily school life and special events. Develops a clear vision for establishing a supportive culture of high expectations, and engages teachers with the vision.

Has clear classroom routines for learning and behavior, grounded in high expectations. Creates opportunities for students to reflect on, discuss, and practice appropriate behaviors.

Progressing

Engages teachers in developing the school values and building school culture. Establishes systems for teachers and students to reflect on aspects of school culture.

Co-creates classroom culture, values, and goals with students. Models, appreciates, and encourages behavior that embodies the school values. Engages in open and honest discussions on school culture and classroom climate.

Developed

Monitors the development of the school culture. Engages families in building a supportive culture of high expectations. Engages the wider community in the school values and the vision for a supportive culture of high expectations.

Engages with families to support student behavior and learning. Engages them in discussions on school culture and classroom climate.
